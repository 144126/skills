# Naming

Research this skill came from — read it first: [references/naming-first-principles.md](references/naming-first-principles.md)

A name is judged before anyone knows the app, then it is the cue to find the app again. Easy to say reads as familiar, so safe and good. But easy to like is not easy to remember. Pick the point on that line the app needs, and make the sounds and meaning fit the promise.

1. Frame: write the promise in one line. Write the feel: small, fast, sharp, or big, slow, solid. Everyday and trusted, or special and thrilling?
2. Ban the 3 names that come first and anything close to them. Then make 30 or more, each from a different lever, each with a rough probability. Keep the tails (under 10%):
   - a common real word from a far domain
   - a word for the one promise the app will always keep
   - rhyme, alliteration, a repeated vowel or syllable
   - two words compounded or blended
   - a coined word built from sounds that fit the feel
3. Kill any name that fails:
   - A stranger hears it once. Can they say it and type it?
   - It is a generic term, or close to an existing app name.
   - Its sounds fight the feel. Front vowels (mill, me) = small, fast, sharp. Back vowels (mall, boom) = big, slow, dull. The "yoo" of putrid loses everywhere.
   - It breaks the language's sound rules (rantifanti beats ranthfanth).
   - It is taken where it must live: store, domain, handle.
4. Lean by the job:
   - Trust (money, health, data, everyday tools): common, real, easy words. Hard names read as risky.
   - Told to friends or typed into search: common real words. They are recalled best.
   - Must stand out in a list: a rarer, concrete word, an odd spelling, a hard first sound (k, g, b, p, t, d), a repeated sound. Costs some liking.
   - Premium, special-occasion, or thrill: some difficulty helps. It reads as rare or exciting.
5. Meaning locks the position. A name that states a benefit lifts recall of that benefit and buries the others. If the app may change direction, use a word that does not describe it.
6. Pair it: the name is identity, at most 30 characters in the App Store. The subtitle says what it does.
7. Do not let naming stall launch. The name pulls hardest before people know the app, and weakens after.
8. Show the top 3: why each fits, and each one's weakness. The user's stated taste and tiebreakers win.
