# Video edit

Research this skill came from — read it first: [references/video-edit-first-principles.md](references/video-edit-first-principles.md) (motion, color, and shape depth: [references/aesthetics-motion-color-shape.md](references/aesthetics-motion-color-shape.md))

The viewer predicts the next moment. Cut where they expect a change, or break the guess so the next beat explains it.

Cut points
- Cut when an action ends or a new one starts, not mid-thought.
- Start the new shot with motion. Overlap the action 1-2 frames.
- Exits: cut on the frame the subject fully leaves. Entries: subject fully in view at once.
- Put the new shot's focus where the eye already is, or at center.
- Keep sound running across the cut (J/L cut, room tone, music). Silence exposes cuts.

Music
- Cut on beats and on motion turning points (top or bottom of a gesture).
- Put the cut on the beat frame or 1-2 frames early. Never more than 1 frame late.
- Mostly on the beat, a few off-beat. Hold through the build, cut hard on the drop.

Pace
- Shot length follows content: wide, new, or complex holds longer; close-ups and repeats cut sooner.
- Mix long and short shots, never a flat metronome. Speed up into the climax, slow after it.
- Open on a question the viewer is half sure of. Surprise early, keep joy rising.

Speech
- Gaps between speakers: about 200 ms (range -100 to 500 ms).
- Leave a short pause before a key word.
- Jump cuts inside one speaker's talk are fine while speech flows. Never cut away for a very short line.
- Captions: up to 20 characters per second.

Meaning
- The shot before colors the next one (Kuleshov). Match cuts: same framing or same motion.
- Break a rule (180 degrees, continuity) only on purpose: it costs clarity, not liking.

Check: watch at full speed with sound. Every cut you notice by accident is a fix.
